# SAL Discord Moderation Guidelines

**Philosophy:** We're building community, not enforcing rules. Moderation is about maintaining the space where SAL's mission can thrive—not about policing.

---

## Core Principle

**Assume good intent. Extend grace. Correct rarely. Remove only when necessary.**

The goal is to create psychological safety where people feel welcome to ask questions, share vulnerabilities, and learn. Heavy-handed moderation contradicts that mission.

---

## What Moderators Do

### 1. Welcome (Active, Ongoing)
- Greet every new member in #introductions
- Thank them specifically for what they shared
- Make them feel seen and welcome
- If appropriate, reply to their introduction with warmth
- Model vulnerability: share moderator's own first-steps

### 2. Facilitate (Attentive)
- Answer questions thoughtfully or invite others to help
- Repost excellent questions from #ask-anything into relevant channels
- Flag interesting discussions for highlighting
- Connect people across conversations
- Uplift quieter members

### 3. Protect (Strategic)
- Monitor for fear-mongering or catastrophizing (rare, but address when it happens)
- Watch for MLM/sales pitches or spam (remove immediately)
- Catch personal attacks (address privately first)
- Flag unsourced claims presented as fact (invite discussion, not accusation)
- Check for conspiracy theories (gentle redirection to evidence)

### 4. Celebrate (Regular)
- Highlight member contributions in #wins-and-progress
- Create member spotlights in #member-stories
- Acknowledge learning moments
- Share wins, no matter how small

## How to Handle Common Situations

### Situation: New member asks a "basic" question

**Response:** Answer warmly and thoroughly. Thank them for asking.
- *"Great question. Most people don't understand this. Here's the short version..."*
- This is your opportunity to model that no question is stupid.

### Situation: Someone posts fear-mongering content

**Response:** Private message first. Assume good intent. Ask about their source.
- *"Hi [name], I saw your post in #market-discussion about [claim]. Can you share your source? We want to make sure we're working with verified information."*
- If they have evidence, ask them to reframe it with context
- If they don't, gently redirect: *"Let's stick to sourced info here. You have concerns about [real issue]—let's dig into that with data."*
- Only remove if they refuse to engage

### Situation: Someone makes a personal attack

**Response:** Take to DM immediately. Don't escalate in public.
- *"Hey, I noticed your comment in [channel] came across as attacking. I don't think that was your intent. Can we talk about what you were trying to say?"*
- Listen. They might have legitimate frustration.
- If they're genuinely attacking, explain community values
- Warn once. Remove on second offense.

### Situation: Someone shares conspiracy theory or unsourced claim

**Response:** Curious, not corrective. Invite evidence.
- *"That's an interesting claim. What's the evidence you're working from? Help me understand the connection."*
- If they have evidence, great. Discuss.
- If not: *"Got it. We want to make sure everything here is evidence-based. Can you find a source? Happy to discuss once we have data."*
- Keep tone collaborative, not authoritative

### Situation: MLM pitch or sales promotion

**Response:** Remove immediately. No warning needed.
- Delete the message
- Private message: *"SAL isn't the space for sales or MLM promotion. We're focused on community and learning."*
- That's it.

### Situation: Member hasn't posted in a while (Week 3+)

**Response:** Reach out. They might have encountered friction.
- *"Hi [name], I haven't seen you in discussions lately. Everything okay? Would love to hear how you're thinking about these issues."*
- Sometimes people go quiet because they felt judged. Reopen the door.
- Sometimes they're just busy. That's fine too.

### Situation: Two members disagree (respectfully)

**Response:** Let it play out. This is healthy.
- Don't jump in unless one person attacks the other
- Disagreement is healthy. Evidence-based disagreement is better.
- If it's getting heated: *"I appreciate you both bringing different perspectives. Let's focus on evidence here. What data are we each working from?"*

### Situation: Someone says "This is just like [historical disaster]"

**Response:** Acknowledge the concern, avoid the catastrophizing.
- *"I get the parallel you're drawing. What's actually happening here is [specific, evidence-based]. Here's how this time is different [key differentiators]."*
- People make historical connections when anxious. Don't shame them.
- Provide reality-grounding with data.

### Situation: A member is vulnerable or sharing a struggle

**Response:** Validate. Connect to others if appropriate.
- *"Thank you for sharing that. A lot of people are feeling this way right now."*
- Don't try to "fix" their anxiety. Just acknowledge it.
- If relevant, invite others to share experience in the thread
- This is exactly the kind of vulnerability that builds community

## What NOT to Do

### Don't
- ❌ Jump into every conversation to correct minor things
- ❌ Over-moderate for perfection in spelling/grammar
- ❌ Remove posts just because you disagree
- ❌ Create an authoritarian vibe ("I'm the expert, you're wrong")
- ❌ Let fear-mongering run unchecked (there's a balance)
- ❌ Punish people for being new or inexperienced
- ❌ Punish people for being anxious
- ❌ Use moderation to advance your own opinions
- ❌ Leave spam or MLM pitches up "to make a point"
- ❌ Post frequently as "the authority"

### Do
- ✅ Assume good intent
- ✅ Extend grace
- ✅ Address privately first
- ✅ Model the values you want to see
- ✅ Celebrate contribution
- ✅ Keep psychological safety as the north star
- ✅ Use light touch most of the time
- ✅ Build culture through example, not enforcement

## Role Assignments & Permissions

### Moderator Role
- **Can:** Pin messages, delete/edit posts, manage channels, manage roles, mute members, kick members
- **Should:** Use sparingly. Default to dialogue first.
- **Red lines:** Remove MLM/spam/extreme attacks without discussion. Use dialogue for everything else.

### Verified Role
- **Can:** Post in all user channels, participate fully
- **Granted:** After member introduces themselves in #introductions
- **Automatic:** No manual approval needed. Granted by role permissions.

### Community Member Role (Default)
- **Can:** View main channels, post in #ask-anything, #introductions, #technical-help only
- **Restrictions:** Can't see high-volume channels until they introduce selves (prevents drive-by trolling)
- **Conversion:** Automatically gets Verified role after they participate in #introductions

## Escalation Path (If Needed)

### Level 1: Question / Curiosity
- DM the person
- Assume good intent
- Ask them to help you understand
- Give them chance to edit or clarify

### Level 2: Concern / Boundary-Setting
- If they don't respond or double down on problematic behavior
- Explain SAL community values
- Be specific about what violated the values
- Give them chance to course-correct

### Level 3: Warning
- If behavior continues after Level 2
- Formal DM: "This behavior violates SAL community values. Next instance results in removal."
- Document the behavior (keep record)

### Level 4: Removal
- Remove message or member
- Can be temporary (24-hour mute) or permanent (kick/ban)
- Only use for: repeated attacks, refusal to engage, spam, or safety violations
- Send a final DM explaining the removal and SAL's door is open if they want to revisit

## FAQ for Moderators

**Q: What if someone asks a question I don't know the answer to?**
A: Say so. Model intellectual humility. *"Great question—I'm not sure. Anyone else know this?"* This is perfect.

**Q: What if someone disagrees with the SAL approach (collective resilience vs. prepping)?**
A: That's fine. Disagreement is healthy. Engage with their ideas. If they're being dismissive of SAL's values, remind them gently: *"We're taking a collective approach here. That works for us. If that's not your thing, no judgment—this might not be the space for you."*

**Q: How do I know if something is "fear-mongering"?**
A: Ask yourself: Is this claim sourced? Does it present catastrophe as inevitable without evidence? Does it seem designed to maximize anxiety rather than clarity? If yes to all three, it's worth addressing. When in doubt, ask for the source.

**Q: What if a moderator violates community values?**
A: Call them in. Same standard applies. Use the escalation path. Moderators model the culture—they have to exemplify it or lose credibility.

**Q: Should I remove disagreement?**
A: No. Unless it's attacking/dismissive. Disagreement is health. Help it be *productive* disagreement. *"I see you two have different takes. What data is each person working from?"*

**Q: How often should I post as a moderator?**
A: Not often. Your role is to facilitate, not to be the voice. Post occasionally (resource shares, decision-making frameworks, member spotlights), but let the community drive the conversation. Aim for 20% moderator posts, 80% community posts.

**Q: What if someone is spreading medical/health misinformation?**
A: Remove it immediately and explain why in a DM. Health claims need evidence. No debate needed. *"We can't host medical claims without strong evidence. For health questions, please consult medical professionals."*

**Q: What if the community gets too big and we can't keep up?**
A: That's success. Start recruiting trusted members as co-moderators. Look for members who exemplify SAL values. Share the load. The culture should sustain itself by then.

## Moderator Self-Check (Weekly)

Ask yourself:

- ☐ Am I assuming good intent in conflicts?
- ☐ Am I addressing things privately first?
- ☐ Am I celebrating contribution?
- ☐ Am I welcoming newcomers?
- ☐ Am I modeling the behavior I want to see?
- ☐ Am I removing spam/MLM immediately?
- ☐ Am I over-moderating somewhere?
- ☐ Are people feeling psychologically safe to ask questions?
- ☐ Is the community self-sustaining (less moderator input needed)?
- ☐ Do people feel heard?

If you're not seeing mostly ✓ marks, recalibrate.

## The SAL Moderation Compass

When in doubt, ask:

**Does this post/behavior:**
1. Violate psychological safety? → Address it
2. Break trust in sourced information? → Address it
3. Attack another person? → Address it privately first
4. Promote MLM/spam/sales? → Remove immediately
5. Just disagree with you? → Let it stand

**Use 80/20 rule:**
- 80% of the time: Facilitate, celebrate, welcome
- 20% of the time: Correct, redirect, remove

This maintains the culture without becoming authoritarian.

---

**Remember:** You're building the space where people feel less alone and more capable. Everything you do should serve that mission.
